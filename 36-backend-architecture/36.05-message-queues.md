### Broker Comparison (2026)

| Factor | RabbitMQ | Kafka | SQS |
|--------|----------|-------|-----|
| **Throughput** | 10k-50k msg/s | 100k+ msg/s | 3k msg/s (std), 300 msg/s (FIFO) |
| **Message size** | Up to 128 MB | Up to 1 MB (configurable) | 256 KB |
| **Operability** | Medium (cluster, quorum queues) | Low (complex) | Very high (zero ops) |
| **Replay** | Limited | Excellent | Limited (3 days retention) |
| **Per-key ordering** | Limited | Excellent | FIFO queues only |
| **Pub/sub** | Yes (exchanges) | Yes (consumer groups) | Needs SNS |
| **Cost** | Self-hosted | Self-hosted | Pay-per-use |

**SQS wins on operability/durability/delay/cost** - it is the pragmatic default for a small team on AWS. **Kafka wins on replay/ordering/throughput/pub-sub** - the choice if you expect to grow into a streaming platform or need replay. **RabbitMQ is the balanced middle** with the unique priority-queue feature.

### When Each Is the Right Answer
- **RabbitMQ** - Complex routing (topic/fanout/header exchanges), per-message priority, RPC-style request/reply, moderate throughput, battle-tested traditional broker. Classic fit for task/work queues with rich routing.
- **Kafka** - High-throughput event streaming, replayability, multiple independent consumer groups, strict per-key ordering, log/CDC/analytics pipelines. Overkill for a simple work queue; perfect when the queue is the data backbone.
- **SQS** - You are on AWS, want zero ops, at-least-once with built-in visibility-timeout redelivery, native delay and DLQ, and pay-per-use. The default cloud work queue; pair with SNS for fan-out.

### Delivery Semantics

| Semantics | Guarantee | Cost |
|-----------|-----------|------|
| **At-most-once** | Message delivered 0 or 1 time | Fastest, risk of loss |
| **At-least-once** | Message delivered 1+ times | Requires idempotent consumers |
| **Exactly-once** | Message delivered exactly 1 time | Expensive, complex |

**The practical answer: at-least-once + idempotent consumers.**

---

### Deeper Explanation: Queue Semantics Decide Your Failure Handling

A queue is a buffer with delivery semantics, and the semantics decide your code:

| Model | Guarantee | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| At-most-once | May be lost | Only for non-critical, repeatable work |
| At-least-once | May be duplicated | Idempotent handlers, dedupe by id |
| Exactly-once (effective) | Dedupe + transactional publish | Requires idempotency + outbox, not magic |

Practical matching: background emails and thumbnail generation tolerate at-least-once; payment effects require idempotency keys; analytics tolerate loss. Also decide ordering needs per queue (per-entity ordering via partition/group keys), visibility timeouts (longer than the worst-case processing time, or work is retried while still running), retry policies with backoff and a max attempt count, and dead-letter handling with an operational owner.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Visibility timeout shorter than processing time.** The job runs twice concurrently â€” the classic queue bug.
- **No idempotency.** Retries duplicate side effects.
- **Unbounded retries.** A poison message loops forever; cap attempts and dead-letter.
- **One queue for everything.** Fast and slow jobs starve each other; separate by latency class.
- **No monitoring of depth and age.** Queue depth and oldest-message age are the two metrics that predict outages.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **No DLQ** → poison messages block the queue forever. Always configure a dead-letter queue.
- **No visibility timeout** → messages are reprocessed while still being processed.
- **No idempotency** → at-least-once delivery means duplicates. Use idempotency keys.
- **Kafka for simple work queue** → overkill. Use SQS or RabbitMQ.
- **SQS for replay** → SQS retention is limited (3 days). Use Kafka if you need replay.
