## 36.1 Monolith

### Subtopic Tree

### What a Monolith Is
A monolith packages the entire application - all business logic, all domains, all routes - into a **single deployable unit**. It runs as one process, connects to one (or few) databases, and is deployed as a whole. Modern monoliths remain a strong choice for smaller teams and simpler enterprise requirements.

### Layered Architecture (The Classic Monolith Structure)
```text
┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Presentation Layer (HTTP handlers)  │
├─────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Application Layer (use cases)       │
├─────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Domain Layer (business logic)       │
├─────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Infrastructure Layer (DB, APIs)     │
└─────────────────────────────────────┘
```
Dependencies point **inward**: presentation depends on application, application depends on domain, domain depends on nothing. Infrastructure implements domain interfaces.

### When a Monolith Is Right
✅ MVPs and prototypes - ship fast, iterate quickly
✅ Small teams (<10 engineers) - no coordination overhead
✅ Simple domains - CRUD apps, internal tools, early-stage products
✅ Limited DevOps/SRE bandwidth - no distributed systems complexity
✅ When you don't know your domain boundaries yet

---

### Deeper Explanation: A Monolith Is A Deployment Property, Not A Design

"Monolith" describes one deployable unit; it says nothing about internal structure. The failure mode people fear (a tangled ball of mud) comes from missing module boundaries, not from being single-process. Conversely, a well-structured monolith gives you transactional consistency, simple debugging, cheap refactors, and one deploy pipeline â€” advantages that are hard to overstate for a small team.

Make the monolith good on purpose: separate modules by domain, give each module its own data access layer, forbid cross-module table reads, and treat module interfaces as public APIs (typed, documented, versioned). Then the eventual extraction is mechanical rather than archaeological.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Domain logic in HTTP handlers.** It blocks reuse and testing and couples everything to the transport.
- **A shared "utils" module.** It becomes a magnet for unrelated coupling; prefer domain-named modules.
- **Direct cross-module database access.** The single biggest obstacle to later extraction.
- **No module interface discipline.** Without it, refactors ripple everywhere and teams cannot work in parallel.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **No internal boundaries** → "big ball of mud." Everything imports everything. Refactoring becomes impossible.
- **One change forces full redeploy** → a typo in a marketing page requires redeploying the entire backend.
- **Scaling the whole app to scale one feature** → "In a monolithic model, scaling often means scaling the entire application even when only one feature needs more capacity".
- **No modular structure** → the monolith becomes unmaintainable. A well-structured monolith with proper modular boundaries, automated testing, and deployment automation can scale further than most teams think.
