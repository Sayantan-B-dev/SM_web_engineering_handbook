### The Six-Layer Wallet UX Stack
Think of Ethereum's UX evolution as a building, where each layer solves one problem:

| Layer | Standard | Problem Solved |
|-------|----------|----------------|
| 1-2 | `window.ethereum` + EIP-1193 | Standard provider interface |
| 3 | EIP-6963 | Multi-wallet discovery |
| 4 | ERC-4337 | Account abstraction |
| 5 | EIP-7702 | Bridge for existing EOAs |
| 6 | Reown (WalletConnect) | Cross-device relay |

### EIP-1193: The Foundation
In the beginning, there was `window.ethereum` - the basic JavaScript object that wallets inject into your browser. When a dApp says "Connect Wallet," it's looking for this object. The problem: every wallet implemented it slightly differently. **EIP-1193 fixed this by creating a standard interface**.

### EIP-6963: The Multi-Wallet Problem
You have MetaMask installed. You also have Rabby. You click "Connect Wallet" on a dApp. Which wallet opens? Usually MetaMask because it's aggressive about claiming `window.ethereum`. Your other wallet gets ignored. **EIP-6963 introduces wallet discovery** - instead of wallets fighting for the same global object, dApps can ask "What wallets are available?" and show a proper list.

### ERC-4337: Account Abstraction
Right now, your Ethereum account is controlled by a private key. Lose it? Gone. Get phished? Gone. Want to pay gas in USDC instead of ETH? Can't. **ERC-4337 introduces smart accounts - wallets that are actually smart contracts**. This unlocks:

- **Gas sponsorship**: Apps can pay your gas fees
- **Batched transactions**: One signature for multiple actions
- **Social recovery**: Recover your wallet through trusted contacts

**Real example**: Before ERC-4337, swapping on Uniswap required (1) approve token, (2) confirm approval, (3) submit swap, (4) confirm swap. **Four popups. Four chances to abandon the transaction. With smart accounts: one signature. Done.**

### EIP-7702: The Bridge
ERC-4337 is great for new users. But what about the millions of people with existing wallets? They can't just migrate their accounts, their history, their tokens are all tied to their current address. **EIP-7702 lets existing wallets temporarily act like smart accounts. You keep your address. You keep your keys. But you get smart account features when you need them. Think of it as a software upgrade for your existing wallet**.

### Slice Wallet (2026 Example)
Slice Wallet is a self-custodial smart wallet rooted in a passkey. A WebAuthn credential controls a Kernel v4 account on **ERC-4337 EntryPoint v0.9**, with the same counterfactual address on Ethereum, and supports EIP-1193/EIP-6963/EIP-5792 provider standards.

### Deeper Explanation: The Wallet Is The Auth Layer And The Risk Surface

A wallet holds keys and signs; your app never sees the private key. That makes wallet integration an *identity + signing* problem, with a user experience dominated by switching networks, approval prompts, and the difference between connecting (read) and signing (write).

Practical knowledge: EIP-1193 defines the provider interface (`request({ method, params })`), EIP-6963 improves discovery of multiple injected wallets (the old `window.ethereum` race is a real bug source), WalletConnect handles mobile wallets via QR/deep links, and smart accounts (ERC-4337) enable sponsored gas, session keys, and recovery. The user-facing rules: never request a signature you cannot explain, always show the network, and treat every rejection as a normal, non-error outcome.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Assuming `window.ethereum` exists or is the only wallet.** Feature-detect, support EIP-6963 discovery, and provide a WalletConnect path.
- **Long-lived signature requests.** Dangling prompts confuse users; cancel cleanly on unmount and on network change.
- **Not handling account/chain change events.** Users switch accounts mid-session; your UI must reset state.
- **Requesting infinite token approvals.** Ask for the exact amount with a clear explanation.
- **Surfacing wallet errors as app errors.** "User rejected" is expected behaviour, not a bug report.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Seed phrases as the only recovery** → 12-word seed phrases are a UX disaster. ERC-4337 enables social recovery and passkeys.
- **No EIP-6963 support** → users with multiple wallets can't choose which to connect. MetaMask hijacks `window.ethereum`.
- **Assuming ERC-4337 works everywhere** → it requires a bundler, paymaster, and EntryPoint contract. Not all chains support it.
- **EIP-7702 security considerations** → existing EOAs "temporarily" acting like smart accounts introduces new attack surfaces.
