### What RPC Is
RPC (Remote Procedure Call) is how your dApp talks to the blockchain. A node exposes a JSON-RPC interface; your frontend sends method calls (`eth_call`, `eth_sendRawTransaction`, `eth_getBalance`) and receives responses. The RPC provider is the **first point of failure** for any dApp.

The **more consequential finding** is a single-region tail event: dRPC's US p99 spikes to 1,370ms - more than 6× its own DE p99 - pulling its composite score to 0.541, over six times Chainstack's. A blended global average would report this as "dRPC is somewhat slower"; the regional breakdown shows it is Chainstack-competitive in two regions and **badly degraded in the third**.

### Why Tail Latency Matters on Ethereum
Ethereum's size raises the cost of getting this wrong. A tail-latency event on a thin chain affects a handful of early integrators; **a tail-latency event on Ethereum can sit behind a production trading system, a wallet backend, or an indexer serving thousands of users**.

### Deeper Explanation: RPC Is A Trust And Reliability Boundary

Your RPC endpoint is a third-party server that answers chain queries and broadcasts transactions. It can be rate-limited, stale, censoring, or simply down â€” and it is also a privacy leak, since it sees the user's IP and queries. So treat it like any external dependency: multiple providers, health checks, timeouts, retries with backoff, and a user-visible degraded state.

Design notes that matter in production: use dedicated providers with keyed rate limits rather than public endpoints; batch and cache reads (`eth_call` results, block data) because most reads are repeated; read from the same block number across related queries to avoid inconsistent snapshots; for writes, broadcast to more than one provider when a transaction is critical, since propagation differs.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **A single public RPC endpoint.** Rate limits and outages become your outage.
- **API keys exposed in the client.** Providers require keys; proxy through your backend or use domain-restricted keys.
- **Reading at "latest" across multiple calls.** Inconsistent states; pin a block number.
- **No caching.** Repeated identical reads burn quota and add latency.
- **Ignoring provider privacy.** Queries reveal user activity; a proxy or a privacy-friendly provider is a product decision.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Single RPC provider** → if your provider goes down, your dApp goes down. Use failover with multiple providers.
- **Ignoring tail latency** → a provider with great median latency can have a 1,370ms p99 in one region.
- **No API key rotation** → leaked API keys can be abused. Rotate keys and set spending limits.
- **Self-hosted node without failover** → your node is a single point of failure. Use a hosted provider as backup.
- **Not benchmarking regionally** → a provider that's fast in the US may be slow in Singapore. Benchmark in your users' regions.
