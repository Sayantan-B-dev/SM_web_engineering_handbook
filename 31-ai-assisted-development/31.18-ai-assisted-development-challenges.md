### The Failure Modes
A systematic mapping study of LLM agents in software development identified recurring failure modes:
- **Illusion of correctness** in generated code
- **Fragile test suites**
- **Overly permissive CI/CD configurations**
- **Code origin and license compatibility risks**

### The Productivity-Quality Paradox
Key metrics reveal:
- **4× increase in code duplication** (violating DRY principles)
- **Doubling of code churn** compared to 2021 baselines
- A **"Verification Bottleneck"** has emerged
- **Security remains a critical failure point**, with over **51% of AI-authored code containing vulnerabilities**

### Developers Feel More Productive, Performance Declines
Large-scale studies document a troubling pattern: developers report feeling more productive while **measured performance declines** (METR 2025), and individual output rises while **organizational delivery velocity stagnates** (Faros AI 2025).

### AI Coding Agents Operate Tactically
AI coding agents operate **tactically rather than strategically**. While highly productive in isolated tasks, their limited context windows and behavioral unpredictability lead to **technical debt accumulation and gradual architectural degradation**.

### ACM's TechBrief Warning
AI-generated code introduces **security vulnerabilities, increases technical debt, and produces code that can be difficult to maintain**. Risks include security vulnerabilities inherited from training data, inconsistent or missing testing, and systems that become difficult for humans to review or maintain over time.

### Cost as the Top Barrier
Cost is now the top barrier to broader AI adoption, cited by **28%** of developers as the primary obstacle to wider AI agent use.

---

### Deeper Explanation: The Real Costs Show Up Later

The challenges are mostly second-order, which is why they surprise teams:

- **Review bandwidth becomes the bottleneck.** Authoring speeds up 2-5Ã-; review does not. If review capacity stays fixed, quality drops or the queue grows.
- **Homogenised, confidently wrong code.** The same training distribution produces the same plausible answer in different teams â€” including the same subtle mistakes.
- **Knowledge erosion.** Engineers who do not read what they merge lose debugging ability; juniors skip the failure-recovery lessons.
- **Test debt and weakened assertions.** Generated tests can look comprehensive while asserting nothing meaningful.
- **Attribution and compliance.** Licences, provenance, and disclosure policies vary by organisation and jurisdiction.
- **Cost creep.** Per-seat plus per-token billing scales with usage, not value.

Mitigations are organisational: review budgets, mandated reading of diffs, mutation testing on critical suites, provenance records, and cost dashboards per team.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Measuring only authoring speed.** The system's throughput is limited by review and validation.
- **No skill floor.** Teams need people who can diagnose what the model wrote, especially during incidents.
- **Ignoring licensing provenance.** Public-code filtering is a mitigation, not a guarantee.
- **No cost ownership.** Token spend without a budget becomes a surprise at quarter end.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Illusion of correctness** → AI code looks right but isn't. Verification is the bottleneck.
- **4× code duplication** → AI generates duplicate logic. Enforce DRY in review.
- **Architectural degradation** → AI operates tactically, not strategically. Humans must maintain architectural vision.
- **Feeling productive vs. measured performance** → METR 2025 found developers feel more productive while measured performance declines. Track real metrics.
- **Cost as a barrier** → 28% of developers cite cost as the top obstacle. Monitor token spend (up 10-13×).
