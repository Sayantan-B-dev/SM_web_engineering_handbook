### Deeper Explanation: Flows Reward Context Discipline

Editor agents with "flow" style multi-step editing put the emphasis on maintaining context across a change: they track which files matter and keep a running model of the task. The payoff appears on medium-sized refactors; the failure mode appears when the context window fills with irrelevant files and the agent starts making plausible, disconnected edits.

The discipline: start from a single explicit goal, keep the relevant files open, and check in after each coherent step rather than at the end of a long autonomous run. For refactors, ask for a plan first â€” rewriting the plan is far cheaper than reverting a half-applied change.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Long autonomous stretches before review.** Reverting twenty small edits costs more than reviewing five.
- **Context bloat.** Thousands of indexed files dilute the important ones; scope the change to directories.
- **Uncommitted experiments.** Commit a baseline first so any AI change is diffable and revertible.
- **Assuming consistency with your patterns.** Give it the convention file; do not rely on inference.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Assuming Windsurf is a Cursor replacement** → both are AI-native IDEs, but Windsurf's Cascade agent has a different workflow.
