### Multi-Agent Bug Hunting (Anthropic's Test)
Anthropic planted 70 bugs in 116,000 lines of code. A **single agent** found 27 bugs (best result). A **multi-agent team** found 66. This demonstrates that parallel specialist agents (logic, security, regressions) working in concert are dramatically more effective than a single agent.

### DigitalOcean Signals
Signals watches production traffic on DigitalOcean Managed Agents and DigitalOcean Inference Engine, and automatically highlights potential problems. Simulations let you test the fix before it ships.

### Deeper Explanation: Debugging Is Hypothesis Management

AI is strongest at generating hypotheses and reading unfamiliar code, and weakest at confirming them â€” confirmation needs the real runtime, real data, and real logs. The productive division of labour: let the model propose three plausible causes and the exact experiment for each; you run the experiment and feed back the result.

This works because debugging is mostly search: reading stack traces, grepping call sites, hypothesising about state, and bisecting change history. Models are fast at all of those, provided you supply the evidence (the error, the failing input, the recent diff) rather than describing it from memory.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Accepting a plausible-sounding root cause.** Without an experiment that confirms it, you patch symptoms.
- **No reproduction.** Ask for a minimal failing case first; a fix without a reproduction is a guess.
- **Pasting secrets or customer data into prompts.** Redact before sharing logs.
- **Losing the hypothesis trail.** Record what was tested and ruled out; the next session should not repeat it.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Single-agent debugging** → 27 bugs found vs. 66 with multi-agent. Fan out specialist agents.
- **No production observation** → you can't debug what you can't see. Use Signals or equivalent.
- **No reproduction verification** → agent's claim that a bug is fixed must be verified against the running app.
