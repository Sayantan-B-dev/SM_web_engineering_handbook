### What Devin Is in 2026
Devin is best for **fully autonomous ticket execution**. It accepts issues, plans multi-day work, and opens PRs with minimal human direction. However, autonomous agents show the **weakest results**, merging within 30 days at only **79%** (vs. higher rates for assisted tools).

---

### Deeper Explanation: Autonomy Is A Spectrum You Choose Per Task

Fully autonomous agents (Devin-style) are a workflow choice with a long horizon: they plan, edit, run, and report back with minimal supervision. That makes them valuable for well-bounded, repetitive, or exploratory work, and risky wherever correctness depends on context they cannot infer (product intent, security posture, undocumented invariants).

The reliable pattern is to treat autonomy as a dial per task:

| Task shape | Sensible autonomy |
|---|---|
| Failing test with clear expectations | High â€” let it iterate |
| Dependency upgrade with a lockfile | Medium â€” verify diffs, no new deps |
| Feature with product ambiguity | Low â€” human designs, agent implements slices |
| Schema/infra change | Very low â€” human writes the plan and runs the migration |

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Setting autonomy by team policy instead of task shape.** The right dial is per task, not per org.
- **Reviewing only the final artifact.** Autonomy requires reading intermediate tool calls and decisions.
- **No cost or time ceiling.** Long runs need explicit budgets.
- **Letting it write the acceptance tests it is judged by.** Human ownership of the test contract is essential.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Using Devin for ambiguous tasks** → Devin works best with well-scoped, independent tickets.
- **Assuming autonomy = reliability** → Devin has the weakest merge rate (79%). Review its PRs carefully.
- **No guardrails** → autonomous agents need policy controls, cost caps, and human approval for destructive actions.
