## 44.9 Stakeholder Management

### Subtopic Tree
- Identifying stakeholders (technical and non-technical)
- Conflicting views and tradeoff analysis
- Participative decision-making
- Governance meetings (steering committees)
- Technical authority vs stakeholder negotiation
- Communication at the right level of detail

### The Stakeholder Challenge
Requirements are often held by different stakeholders, who may have conflicting views. Tradeoff analysis is an essential activity for comparing, prioritizing, and deciding among different requirements or design options.

### The Governance Meeting
A governance meeting - or steering committee - solves these challenges by providing a forum for key stakeholders to **evaluate, prioritize, and approve changes and projects in a structured way**, ensuring budget and technical resources are spent on high-value initiatives.

### The Technical Authority Trap
Research on complex system design reveals a **static vs dynamism paradox**: technical authority pre-emptively frames deliberation by embedding feasibility constraints. While value negotiation is formally open, it's materially constrained by pre-existing hierarchical priorities.

### Participative Decision-Making
Participative decision-making allows stakeholders to take an active role in the decision-making process rather than simply providing comment on proposals.

---

### Deeper Explanation: Different Audiences, Different Questions

Technical decisions involve stakeholders with different concerns: engineering (maintainability, effort), product (time-to-market, user impact), operations (reliability, cost), security (risk), and leadership (cost, risk, strategy). The same decision needs a different framing for each â€” and the framing must be honest, not tailored to please.

The mechanics: identify who is affected and who can veto, brief the affected people before the formal decision (no surprises in the meeting), present one recommendation rather than an open menu, and record the decision and its rationale so absent stakeholders can catch up asynchronously.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Deciding in a vacuum then announcing.** It generates resistance and re-litigation.
- **Presenting options without a recommendation.** It looks like abdication and stalls the decision.
- **Jargon in a cross-functional setting.** Say the user and cost impact, not the acronyms.
- **No written record.** Verbal agreement evaporates.
- **Ignoring the veto holder.** Identify constraints early rather than discovering them after work begins.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Technical authority dominates** → stakeholders feel excluded. Their constraints aren't surfaced.
- **No governance meeting** → decisions are made in silos. Budget is spent on low-value initiatives.
- **Stakeholders only comment, never decide** → decisions lack buy-in.
