## 44.4 Decision Matrix & Weighted Scoring

### Subtopic Tree
- Multi-dimensional weighted scoring
- Criteria weighting (distribute 100 points)
- Scoring scale (1-5)
- Evidence tagging: [CODE], [DOC], [BENCHMARK], [INFERENCE], [ASSUMPTION]
- Sensitivity analysis (which weight changes would flip the ranking)
- Decision boundary (how close are the top options)

### The Weighted Scoring Procedure
A weighted scoring model ranks options by scoring each one against a set of criteria, multiplying each score by the criterion's weight, and adding the results.

```text
Criteria (weighted):           Option A    Option B    Option C
Performance (30)               4 (120)     5 (150)     3 (90)
Learning curve (25)            5 (125)     2 (50)      4 (100)
Ecosystem (20)                 4 (80)      5 (100)     3 (60)
Bundle size (15)               3 (45)      2 (30)      5 (75)
TypeScript support (10)        5 (50)      4 (40)      5 (50)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Total                          420         370         375
```

### Evidence Tagging
Every score must be backed by an evidence tag - never unsubstantiated:
- **[CODE]**: verified in a proof-of-concept
- **[DOC]**: documented in official documentation
- **[BENCHMARK]**: measured performance
- **[INFERENCE]**: reasoned from related evidence
- **[ASSUMPTION]**: believed but not verified

### Sensitivity Analysis
After scoring, perform sensitivity analysis: **which weight changes would flip the ranking?** Identify the decision boundary - how close are the top options? If options are within 10% of each other, choose what the team knows best.

---

### Deeper Explanation: A Tool For Making Assumptions Visible

A weighted matrix does not decide for you; it forces you to state criteria and weights, exposes disagreement, and reveals when a conclusion rests on one subjective score. Use raw numbers (1-5), state the weights up front, and sanity-check the result: if the winner feels wrong, either a weight or a score is wrong, and discussing which is the productive part.

```text
Criteria (weight)          | Option A | Option B | Option C
Onboarding cost      (30%)  |    4    |    3    |    2
Caching correctness  (25%)  |    5    |    4    |    3
Bundle size impact   (15%)  |    3    |    5    |    4
Migration effort     (20%)  |    3    |    2    |    5
Hiring/ecosystem     (10%)  |    4    |    4    |    3
Weighted total               |  3.85   |  3.45   |  3.30
```

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Weights chosen after scoring.** It is retrofitting a justification; fix weights first.
- **Too many criteria.** Beyond five or six, the matrix becomes unreadable and meaningless.
- **Precision illusions.** 3.85 vs 3.80 is noise; treat near-ties as ties and decide on judgement.
- **Ignoring deal-breakers.** Some criteria are pass/fail (compliance, licence); filter before scoring.
- **No re-scoring when facts change.** Update or supersede the matrix with the new information.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Scoring all criteria equally** → ignores stakeholder priorities. Weight by priority.
- **No sensitivity analysis** → you don't know if the decision is robust under reasonable weight changes.
- **No evidence tags** → scores are opinions, not analysis.
- **Ignoring the decision boundary** → choosing between options that are effectively tied.
