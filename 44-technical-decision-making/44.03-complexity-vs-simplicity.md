### Deeper Explanation: Complexity Is Paid Forever, Capability Is Paid Once

Every abstraction, dependency, and layer has a one-time build cost and a permanent comprehension and maintenance cost. The asymmetry is the argument for simplicity: it is almost always cheaper to add complexity later, when the need is proven, than to remove it.

Two useful distinctions: **essential complexity** (inherent in the problem â€” auth, offline, real-time convergence) versus **accidental complexity** (introduced by the solution â€” layers, indirection, config). And **complexity you can see** (explicit code) versus **complexity you cannot** (invisible state, implicit coupling, magic). The second kind is where bugs live.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Abstractions for one use case.** They add indirection with no leverage.
- **Speculative generality.** "We might need it later" is the most expensive sentence in software.
- **Magic over explicitness.** Frameworks that hide control flow cost debugging hours.
- **Removing simplicity to look sophisticated.** Interview-driven design.
- **Unbounded configuration.** Config surface is complexity you maintain but rarely test.
