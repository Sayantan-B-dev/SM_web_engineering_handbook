### The Risk Matrix
A 5×5 risk matrix is the most common format for technical decisions. Risks are commonly summarized using a "risk cube" or "risk matrix" with scales of one to five.

```text
Consequence →
Probability ↓  1     2     3     4     5
     5         M     H     H     E     E
     4         M     M     H     H     E
     3         L     M     M     H     H
     2         L     L     M     M     H
     1         L     L     L     M     M

L = Low, M = Medium, H = High, E = Extreme
```

### Risk-Informed Decision-Making (RIDM)
RIDM is a crucial approach used by organizations such as NASA, FAA, and NRC to assess and manage risks associated with design choices. The TBfD (Technical Basis for Decision) includes a technical summary, risk analysis framework and methods, and adherence to the risk posture.

Risks belong in the decision document, not in someone's head. For each: what could go wrong, how likely, how bad, early warning signs, and the mitigation or contingency. Then decide which risks you accept â€” accepting a risk explicitly is a decision, with an owner.

The categories worth checking for most frontend decisions: performance (will it meet the budget?), operability (can we debug it?), security (what is the attack surface?), cost (what is the ceiling?), talent (can we maintain it?), vendor (what if it disappears?), and schedule (what if adoption slips?).

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Risk lists without mitigations.** A list of fears is not an assessment.
- **Probability invented to support a decision.** Use ranges and state assumptions.
- **Single-point plans.** Have a fallback for the top two risks.
- **Accepted risks with no owner.** Silent risk becomes surprise risk.
- **No review trigger.** Risks change; revisit at defined checkpoints.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **No risk register** → identified risks are forgotten.
- **Single-dimensional risk assessment** → probability without consequence (or vice versa) misses the full picture.
- **No risk mitigation strategies** → risks are identified but not addressed.
