## Architecture Tradeoffs

### Subtopic Tree
- Architecture as a set of forced choices, not best practices
- The seven forces: scale, latency, cost, consistency, team size, time-to-market, compliance
- Trade-off axes: coupling, complexity, operability, evolution
- Reversibility: one-way vs two-way doors
- Cost of change over time (the interest rate of decisions)
- Recording trade-offs (ADRs, decision matrices)

### The Mental Model
Every architecture decision buys one property by paying with another. Naming both sides is what turns an opinion into a decision. The forces that dominate for React products are few: how many users and how concurrent, how strict the latency budget, how big the team is, how fast the product must change, and what compliance applies.

```text
Decision: render the dashboard on the server (SSR) instead of in the browser

Gain:      first contentful paint under 1 s on mid-tier mobile; better SEO
Pay:       a server runtime to operate, per-request cost, harder caching story
Reversible? Mostly (streaming + islands can be adopted incrementally)
Review trigger: if per-request cost exceeds X/month or SSR latency dominates p95
```

### The Two Questions That Simplify Most Debates
1. **How reversible is this?** Two-way doors deserve minutes, one-way doors deserve weeks.
2. **What breaks first as we grow 10Ã-?** Decisions that survive a 10Ã- change are usually the ones worth making; others are premature.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Best-practice copying.** A pattern that fits a 200-person company is often wrong for a five-person one.
- **Ignoring operability.** Anything you cannot operate (or observe) becomes a liability.
- **Treating all decisions as one-way doors.** Over-analysis stalls delivery.
- **No written trade-off.** The same debate returns every quarter.
- **Ignoring the team in the design.** Architectures are constrained by who maintains them.
