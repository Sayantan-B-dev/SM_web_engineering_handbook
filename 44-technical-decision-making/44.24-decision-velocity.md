### What Decision Velocity Is
Decision velocity refers to the **time it takes for your team to go from asking a strategic question to executing on the answer**. It encompasses defining what you want answered, researching options, weighing viability, and confirming with stakeholders.

### The Decision Velocity Framework
A 10-100 engineer org can burn a full sprint in a two-week approval loop. Decision speed comes from **clear decision rights, fast paths for reversible calls, and a small set of slow paths for real one-way doors**.

### Decentralizing Type 2 Decisions
If a decision is reversible, **push the authority down to the team level immediately**. Type 1 decisions (irreversible) require the slow path. Type 2 (reversible) decisions should be made in hours, not weeks.

### Audit Wait Time, Not Work Time
The bottleneck is rarely the work - it's the waiting. Audit the wait time between steps, not the work time within steps.

---

### Deeper Explanation: Slow Decisions Are A Tax On Everything

The cost of a delayed decision is measured in blocked engineers, work in progress, and context switching. Two practices restore velocity: **disagree and commit** (once a decision is made with adequate input, everyone executes it, and if it proves wrong, you revisit with evidence) and **type-2 decision making** (reversible decisions are made by the person closest to the work without escalation).

The signals that velocity is the problem: the same topic appears in three consecutive meetings, multiple teams build parallel solutions, or work is blocked awaiting a design sign-off that nobody owns.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Consensus as a requirement.** It produces slow, lowest-common-denominator decisions.
- **Endless pilots.** Decision by pilot becomes decision by attrition.
- **No decision owner or deadline.** Discussions drift without a closing date.
- **Reopening settled decisions without new information.** It destroys trust in the process.
- **Escalating reversible decisions.** It congests senior time and slows everything.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Every decision escalated** → the CTO becomes the bottleneck. Decentralize Type 2 decisions.
- **No cap on escalation aging** → decisions sit in queues for weeks. Set a maximum wait time.
- **Confusing work time with wait time** → you optimize the wrong thing.
