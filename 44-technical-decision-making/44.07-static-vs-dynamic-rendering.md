### Deeper Explanation: Freshness Is A Product Decision

Static rendering means the page is built once and served from cache; dynamic means it is rendered per request. The trade is freshness against cost, latency, and cacheability. Most real products are a mix, decided per route and sometimes per section:

- **Static / ISR**: marketing, docs, blog, product listings (with revalidation).
- **Streaming SSR + Suspense**: personalised pages where the shell can be static and the personal parts stream.
- **Dynamic (SSR per request)**: user dashboards, anything with per-user pricing, cart, or permissions.
- **Client-side data for the rest**: after the shell, fetch and cache on the client.

The fresh/stale rule to write down: what is the maximum staleness the product tolerates for each surface? That single question usually decides the mechanism.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Stale personal data served from a shared cache.** The worst class of caching bug.
- **Rebuilding thousands of pages on every deploy.** On-demand revalidation is what makes static viable at scale.
- **Dynamic rendering for absolutely static content.** You pay per request for identical HTML.
- **No invalidation hook from the CMS or database.** Content updates wait for the next build.
- **Caching HTML at the CDN without understanding `Vary` and cookies.** Cross-user leakage.
