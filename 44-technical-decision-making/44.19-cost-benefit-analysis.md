### The Core Financial Metrics
Gartner's 2026 guidance identifies the key trade-off points for architecture decisions:

| Metric | What it measures | When to use |
|--------|-----------------|-------------|
| **ROI** | Expected financial and operational payback | Comparing competing approaches |
| **NPV** | Discounts future benefits and costs to today's budget | Long-horizon decisions |
| **TCO** | Full cost including migration, licensing, maintenance | Build vs buy decisions |
| **BCR** | Benefit-cost ratio for economic viability | Infrastructure investments |
| **Payback Period** | Time to recover the initial investment | Capital allocation |

### Uncertainty in CBA
One of the principal tools to make decisions concerning the best use of limited resources is cost-benefit analysis - a tool which is complicated by uncertainty about future outcomes. Use scenario-based CBA with Monte Carlo simulation to model uncertainty ranges.

---

### Deeper Explanation: Total Cost Of Ownership, Honestly Estimated

Cost-benefit for engineering means comparing the full lifecycle cost (build, migrate, operate, train, maintain, integrate, exit) against benefits that are often non-monetary (velocity, reliability, user experience, risk reduction). Most analyses fail by counting build cost and ignoring operation, or by leaving benefits vague.

A practical structure: effort in engineer-weeks (build + integration + migration + ongoing per month), infrastructure and licence cost per month, and the benefit stated as a measurable change (p95 latency, support tickets, conversion, incident frequency). Then state the payback period and the sensitivity: what happens if adoption takes twice as long?

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Ignoring ongoing costs.** Licences, hosting, and on-call time dwarf many build costs.
- **Monetising everything.** Some benefits (accessibility, compliance) are obligations, not investments.
- **Single-point estimates.** Give ranges and state the assumptions.
- **Sunk cost reasoning.** Past investment is not a reason to continue paying.
- **No payback threshold.** Without one, analysis becomes advocacy.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **No NPV for long-term decisions** → future costs are ignored. A decision that looks cheap today may be expensive in 3 years.
- **No TCO** → licensing costs overshadowed by migration and maintenance.
- **Single-point estimates** → no uncertainty range. Use scenario analysis.
