## 44.0 The Technical Decision Landscape (2026)

### Subtopic Tree
- Definition: the discipline of choosing between competing technical options under uncertainty
- Why 2026 is different: AI-assisted development has weakened the "build" gate
- The three anti-patterns that kill decision-making
- Decision velocity as the new engineering KPI
- The irreversibility spectrum (Type 1 vs Type 2 decisions)

### The Core Mental Model
Technical decision-making is not about finding the "right" answer. It is about **making the best possible choice with incomplete information, documenting the rationale, and knowing when to revisit**. Every architectural decision is a trade-off - make trade-offs explicit, weight criteria by stakeholder priorities, and document not just what you chose, but what you rejected and why.

In 2026, AI-assisted development has fundamentally changed the decision psychology. When you can build something meaningful in an afternoon, the default bias becomes "Why wouldn't we just build it?". That temptation is real - but so is the 10/90 problem: building version one is 10-20% of the total lifecycle effort. The remaining 80-90% is maintenance, security patching, integration upkeep, and the inevitable moment when the person who built it moves on and nobody else understands how it works.

### The Three Anti-Patterns
Three anti-patterns consistently emerge when teams avoid structured decision-making:

1. **No decision at all** - out of fear of making the wrong choice. Analysis paralysis.
2. **Decision without justification** - people don't understand why it was made. The same topic is discussed multiple times.
3. **Decision not captured** - team members forget or don't know the decision was made.

### Decision Velocity
Engineering advantage is increasingly measured by **decision velocity** - the time it takes to go from asking a strategic question to executing on the answer. High-velocity teams make decisions in hours, not weeks. They decentralize Type 2 (reversible) decisions to the team level immediately, and reserve slow paths for Type 1 (irreversible) decisions.

### Deeper Explanation: Decisions Have A Shape

Technical decisions differ along two axes: **reversibility** (how expensive to undo) and **consequence** (how much depends on them). That gives four quadrants with different processes:

| | Low consequence | High consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Reversible** | Decide instantly, individually | Decide fast, document briefly |
| **Irreversible** | Decide deliberately, one owner | Expensive analysis, broad input, ADR |

The discipline is to spend analysis proportionally to reversibility.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Uniform process for all decisions.** It wastes weeks on trivia and rushes the fundamentals.
- **No decision owner.** Consensus without an owner produces drift; name one accountable person.
- **Analysis as avoidance.** Research is sometimes the comfortable substitute for committing.
- **Forgetting the revisit trigger.** Every significant decision should state what would change it.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Analysis paralysis** → comparing 10+ options without constraint filtering. Set a decision deadline.
- **Confirmation bias** → scoring the preferred option favorably without evidence. Tag every score with evidence type.
- **Making irreversible decisions without considering reversibility cost** → Type 1 decisions require more analysis than Type 2.
- **Decision latency as the bottleneck** → velocity isn't the bottleneck; decision latency is.
