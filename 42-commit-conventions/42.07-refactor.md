## refactor

### Subtopic Tree
- What qualifies (structure change with no behaviour change)
- The "no behaviour change" proof (tests, diff review)
- refactor vs perf vs feat
- Scope discipline: one refactor per commit
- Mechanical refactors and codemods
- Renames, extraction, dependency inversion
- Refactors that enable a follow-up feature

### The Mental Model
A `refactor:` asserts **behaviour is unchanged**, which is why tests are the evidence and why the diff should be inspectable. Refactors are most valuable when they are small and mechanical: extract a function, invert a dependency, rename consistently, replace a deprecated API. Large "cleanup" commits are unverifiable and impossible to review properly.

```text
refactor(pricing): extract discount rules into a pure module

No behaviour change: the previous inline logic is moved verbatim into
src/pricing/rules.ts and covered by the existing test suite (unchanged).
Follow-up: feat(pricing) will add tiered discounts in that module.
```

### Additional Deal-Breakers
- **Refactors mixed with behaviour changes.** The safety claim becomes false.
- **Massive rewrite labelled refactor.** Reviewers cannot verify equivalence; split it.
- **Missing tests.** "Behaviour unchanged" then rests on hope.
- **Codemod output committed unverified.** Check the diff, then run the suite.
- **Refactor with no follow-up.** Churn without value; tie it to an enabling change.
