## feat

### The Mental Model
`feat:` announces new capability, and because most tooling maps it to a **minor** version bump, it is a promise that existing behaviour still works. That makes the body important: it should say what the user gains, how it was verified, and anything that changes defaults. A feature that alters existing behaviour in an incompatible way is a `feat!:` with a `BREAKING CHANGE` footer.

```text
feat(checkout): support saved payment methods

Adds a saved-cards picker to the payment step and a corresponding
`payment_method` field in the checkout payload. Server support shipped in
api#310. Defaults to the last-used card for returning customers.

Refs #214
```

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Internal refactors labelled `feat:`.** It promises a capability that does not exist and bumps versions pointlessly.
- **Silent default changes.** Users discover new behaviour in production; document and call out.
- **No tests or a manual-only verification note.** Reviewers cannot confirm the happy path.
- **Breaking changes without the footer.** Automation misses the major bump.
- **Huge multi-feature commits.** They cannot be reverted or bisected independently.
