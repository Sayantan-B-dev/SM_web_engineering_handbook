### The Mental Model
`perf:` exists to separate **performance-only changes** from behaviour changes, which matters because they need different review: a perf change must show measurements, and typically must not alter any observable behaviour other than speed. The commit body should carry the numbers â€” before/after with the measurement method â€” because an unmeasured performance claim is unverifiable.

```text
perf(search): memoise result rows and virtualise the list

Before: 480 ms to render 5 000 rows (React Profiler, mid-tier Android).
After:  38 ms with react-window + memoised rows.
Method: scripts/bench-search.mjs against a fixed 5k-row fixture.
No behaviour change: sorting, filtering and selection are identical.
```

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Unmeasured "performance improvements".** Without numbers they cannot be reviewed or trusted.
- **Behaviour changes smuggled into a perf commit.** Reviewers look at speed, not correctness.
- **Micro-optimisations with no user-visible effect.** Added complexity for nothing.
- **No guard against regression.** Add a budget check in CI or the win decays.
- **Benchmarks on developer machines only.** State the device and profile used.
