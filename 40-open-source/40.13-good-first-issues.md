### The Mental Model
A good first issue is **a small, unambiguous task with a defined finish line and a person willing to review it**. It is not "any issue without an assignee" â€” that framing produces abandoned work and frustrated newcomers. The maintainer's job is to do the scoping work up front and to be responsive during the first PR.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Labelling anything "good first issue".** Unscoped tasks are not beginner-friendly; they are just unowned.
- **No acceptance criteria.** The contributor cannot know when they are done, and review becomes negotiation.
- **No responder.** A first issue with no one available to review kills motivation quickly.
- **Requiring a big architectural decision.** Beginners should not have to design the solution.
- **Leaving stale labels after work starts.** Updating the label keeps the queue useful for the next contributor.
- **Ignoring the first-timer's PR follow-through.** A welcome plus a timely review is what turns a one-off into a maintainer.
