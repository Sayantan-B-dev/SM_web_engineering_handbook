### The Core Mental Model
The frontier in 2026 is not a single technology. It is the **convergence of five previously separate domains**: React rendering, AI inference, edge compute, local-first data, and browser-native ML. The frontend developer of 2026 is not just rendering UI - they are orchestrating AI agents, managing local-first state, deploying to the edge, and observing distributed systems.

2020: React app → API → Database

### Deeper Explanation: Frontier Topics Share One Property

Everything in this chapter has the same shape: it moves capability to where the user is (compiler, worker, device, peer) or moves intelligence into the application (agents, generated UI). That has predictable consequences for adoption: the win is real, and the cost is paid in complexity, safety, and operational maturity â€” not in raw capability.

The evaluation frame that keeps frontier work honest: (1) what constraint does this relieve, measured how? (2) what new failure modes does it introduce, and who owns them? (3) can we ship it behind a flag and roll it back? (4) does the team have the skills to maintain it in six months? Technology that fails those questions becomes an abandoned experiment with debt attached.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Adopting frontier technology without a measured constraint.** Novelty is not a requirement.
- **Ignoring the new failure modes.** Generative UI brings injection; local-first brings merge complexity; edge compute brings cold starts.
- **No rollback path.** Everything here should ship behind a flag.
- **Skills mismatch.** Maintenance cost is the real adoption barrier.
- **Reading the demo, not the operations story.** The interesting part is what happens at 3am.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Treating frontier topics as separate concerns** → they are deeply interconnected. A local-first CRDT app needs edge sync. An AI agent needs observability. Browser ML needs WebGPU and Workers.
- **Adopting without understanding the trade-offs** → every frontier topic has a cost. Edge compute adds cold-start complexity. Local-first adds sync complexity. AI adds hallucination risk.
- **No governance despite adoption** → 94% of engineering leaders say their teams use AI; only 6% have systems to scale and govern it.
