## Technical Debt

### Subtopic Tree
- Debt as a deliberate trade (speed now, interest later)
- Types: deliberate/prudent, accidental/reckless
- Interest: slower changes, more bugs, onboarding friction
- Detection: cycle time, change failure rate, hotspots, bug density
- Prioritising repayment against product work
- Refactoring safely (tests first, incremental, behind flags)
- Preventing new debt: review standards, ADRs, budgets
- Communicating debt to non-engineers (in user and cost terms)

### The Mental Model
Debt is not a synonym for bad code; it is a **loan against future change**. It becomes a problem when it is invisible or unowned, because the interest is paid in every future change. So the discipline is the same as finance: record the loan, know the rate, and schedule repayment against a trigger.

```text
Debt register (keep it short and owned)

Item            | Why it exists           | Interest            | Repay when        | Owner
----------------|-------------------------|---------------------|-------------------|--------
tax table       | shipped market #1 fast  | 1-2 days per market | 3rd market commits| checkout
no e2e for cart | deadline                | ~2 regressions/qtr  | next sprint       | web
legacy adapter  | vendor migration        | extra layer to read | vendor v2 landing | platform
```

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Debt taken silently.** Nobody can plan repayment; it surfaces as an emergency.
- **No repayment trigger or owner.** It compounds indefinitely.
- **Interest invisible in metrics.** Track cycle time and change failure rate per area.
- **Big-bang rewrites as the only repayment plan.** Incremental, test-covered refactors are safer.
- **Refactoring without tests.** You cannot prove behaviour is preserved.
- **All-or-nothing prioritisation.** Small frequent repayments beat a promised "debt quarter".
