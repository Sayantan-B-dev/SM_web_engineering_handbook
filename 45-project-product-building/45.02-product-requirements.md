### The Three Criteria for a Valid Problem
Before a product is built, teams need evidence that the problem is **commercially meaningful**. The goal is to identify customer problems that meet three key criteria: they are **urgent**, **pervasive** across the customer base, and customers are **willing to pay to solve them**.

```text
❌ "People might like a better way to manage their tasks."
   (Not urgent, not pervasive, willingness to pay unproven)

✅ "Freelancers lose $2,000/month in unbilled hours because they track time in spreadsheets."
   (Urgent - money is lost; Pervasive - common across freelancers; Willing to pay - it costs them money)
```

### The Customer Interview Process
The traditional process starts with an initiative manager exploring a defined area of the business, speaking with customers, and identifying problems worth solving. Teams would interview **30-plus customers** and build a list of ideas, then narrow it down to one or two.

Key interview questions:
- What are you doing today to solve this problem?
- What's the hardest part about that?
- How much time/money does it cost you?
- What happens if you don't solve it?

### Separating Assumptions from Verified Insights
Many founders skip validation because they're sure people want their idea. The psychological trap is understandable: after identifying what seems like an obvious problem and envisioning an elegant solution, it feels certain.

| Assumption | Verified Insight |
|------------|-----------------|
| "Users want X feature" | "15 of 20 interviewed users asked for X feature" |
| "Users will pay $10/month" | "8 of 20 users signed a letter of intent at $10/month" |
| "This problem is painful" | "Users currently spend 5 hours/week on manual workarounds" |

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Solutions disguised as requirements.** "Add a carousel" hides the problem and blocks better answers.
- **No acceptance criteria.** "Done" becomes a matter of opinion.
- **No measurement plan.** You cannot verify the outcome, so scope debates recur.
- **Unstated non-goals.** Scope creep arrives disguised as obvious additions.
- **Requirements without edge cases.** Empty states, errors, permissions, and offline behaviour ship undefined.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Falling in love with the solution instead of the problem** → successful entrepreneurs fall in love with their customer's problem. Many founders fall in love with their solution.
- **Skipping validation entirely** → budget waste typically stems from feature bloat and building based on internal bias rather than market demand.
- **No defined success criteria** → you can't know if your MVP worked. Define what "validated" means before you build.
- **Interviewing fewer than 20 customers** → insufficient signal. Qualitative data from 20-30 interviews will save you months of building the wrong thing.
