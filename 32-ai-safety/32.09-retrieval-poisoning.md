## 32.9 Retrieval Poisoning

### Subtopic Tree

### The Threat
RAG systems introduce **compromised data integrity, knowledge base poisoning, indirect prompt injection, search result manipulation, embedding inversion, sensitive information leakage, access control bypass, and supply chain attacks**.

### Continuous Ingestion Vulnerability
Continuous ingestion can expose new RAG content to retrieval **before vetting completes**, creating a **temporal attack surface** that conventional admission decisions do not capture. A fail-closed provisional-visibility protocol admits new content under a deadline, but poisoning exposure remains conditional on verifier correctness.

### Retrieval In-Place Poisoning (RIPE-II)
RIPE-II evaluates IPIs under a realistic content-poisoning threat model. It constructs delimiter-free, position-aware poisoned corpora across multiple domains. **Poisoned documents can reach the final model context in up to 97% of queries and rank first in up to 94% of cases**.

---

### Deeper Explanation: Write Access To Your Corpus Is Write Access To Your Model

If an attacker can place content in the knowledge base, they can influence every answer that retrieves it. The corpus is therefore part of your attack surface: tickets, wikis, comments, filenames, commit messages, and third-party sources all qualify.

Controls that hold:

- **Provenance and trust tiers.** Weight or filter by source; treat user-contributed content as lower trust than curated docs.
- **Ingestion review for high-trust corpora.** Anything that can change financial, legal, or medical answers should be reviewed before indexing.
- **Content sanitisation at ingest** â€” strip invisible unicode, embedded instructions patterns, and remote references.
- **Retrieval filtering and diversity.** Do not let one poisoned document dominate the context; cap per-source contribution and rerank.
- **Monitoring.** Log retrieved ids per answer so a bad answer can be traced to a document, and provide a takedown path.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Indexing user-generated content with no trust distinction.** Anyone who can comment can steer answers.
- **No attribution per chunk.** Without source ids, you cannot diagnose or remediate.
- **Single-document dominance.** One poisoned chunk should not fill the whole context window.
- **No removal workflow.** When a document is found to be contaminated, removal must purge the index and caches.
- **Assuming internal sources are clean.** Insider and accidental poisoning are realistic.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **No corpus vetting** → attackers plant documents through any ingestion channel. Vet all content before it enters the vector store.
- **No provisional visibility** → continuous ingestion exposes unvetted content to retrieval. Use fail-closed provisional visibility.
- **Trusting similarity scores** → cosine similarity underreports semantic compromise by 5×.
- **No access control on the vector store** → embedding inversion can recover original text. Encrypt embeddings.
