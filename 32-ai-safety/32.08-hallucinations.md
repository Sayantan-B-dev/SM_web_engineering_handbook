## 32.8 Hallucinations

### Subtopic Tree
- Definition: model generates false information confidently
- The trade-off: hallucination mitigation vs safety alignment
- Truthfulness assurance architecture (governance, calibrated abstention, safe-default routing)
- Fact-graph feedback loops
- Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) as mitigation
- Hallucination-risk governance

### The Unintended Trade-Off (EACL 2026)
A critical side effect of hallucination mitigation is **weakened refusal behavior**. Increasing factual accuracy often comes at the cost of safety alignment. This arises from **overlapping components in the model that simultaneously encode hallucination and refusal information**, leading alignment methods to suppress factual knowledge unintentionally. Fine-tuning on benign datasets, even when curated for safety, can degrade alignment for the same reason.

### Truthfulness Assurance Architecture
High-stakes AI systems should not merely detect hallucinations after an answer has formed. They should **govern whether a candidate answer is allowed to cross the expression boundary at all**. The architecture combines hallucination-risk governance, calibrated abstention, and safe-default routing.

```text
Candidate answer → Hallucination risk assessment → Calibrated abstention?
  → Yes → Safe-default routing (fallback response)
  → No → Expression boundary → User
```

### Fact-Graph Feedback (FGF)
FGF converts an LLM's initial answer and chain-of-thought into **(subject, relation, object) fact graphs**, then validates them against a knowledge base. On TruthfulQA, HaluEval, and DefAn, FGF achieves **5-20% improvements in truthfulness** and markedly reduces hallucinations.

---

### Deeper Explanation: Design For Wrong Answers, Not Against Them

Hallucinations are a property of the system, not a bug to eliminate. The engineering response is to make wrong answers **detectable, low-consequence, and visibly uncertain**:

- **Ground with retrieval and cite sources**, so claims can be checked; ungrounded factual claims should be rare by design.
- **Constrain the domain**: schemas and enums mean a hallucinated field cannot exist.
- **Add deterministic checks** where truth is checkable â€” compute totals in code, validate ids against the database, verify that every citation resolves.
- **Calibrate the language**: instruct explicit uncertainty, and enforce an "insufficient information" path.
- **Keep a human in the loop** where an error is expensive: medical, legal, financial, or irreversible actions.

Evaluation is the discipline that makes this measurable: a labelled set of prompts with expected behaviours (correct answer, correct refusal, no fabricated citation), run on every prompt/model change.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Presentation that implies certainty.** Uniform styling for verified and unverified content is the most common trust failure.
- **No refusal path.** If "I do not know" is not an allowed output, the model invents.
- **Fabricated citations.** Verify that cited sources exist and contain the claim; a citation-shaped hallucination is worse than none.
- **No evals.** Without a labelled set, quality changes are invisible.
- **Numbers generated by the model.** Arithmetic belongs in code.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Mitigating hallucination without monitoring refusal behavior** → you may weaken safety alignment. Monitor both.
- **No abstention mechanism** → the model answers even when it doesn't know. Implement calibrated abstention.
- **No fact verification** → the model's confident output is treated as truth. Use FGF or RAG for verification.
- **Assuming RAG eliminates hallucination** → RAG grounds responses, but the model can still misinterpret retrieved content.
