### The Defense
```http
X-Frame-Options: DENY
Content-Security-Policy: frame-ancestors 'none';
```

CSP's `frame-ancestors 'none'` is the modern replacement for `X-Frame-Options`. Include both for defense in depth.

---

### Deeper Explanation: Framing Is A UI-Integrity Attack

Clickjacking does not steal data directly; it tricks users into clicking controls they cannot see â€” overlay tricks, transparent frames, a decoy page under your login button. It matters most for state-changing or consent-granting actions (OAuth consent, delete, publish) and for anything with a "click to enable" affordance.

Modern defence is one header with precise semantics: `Content-Security-Policy: frame-ancestors 'none'` (or an allow-list of origins you genuinely embed into). `X-Frame-Options: DENY` still works but cannot express allow-lists; JavaScript frame-busting is unreliable and can be defeated by double framing. For privileged actions, defence-in-depth adds re-authentication, `SameSite` cookies (frames are cross-site, so Lax blocks the cookie), and confirmation UI that cannot be auto-clicked.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **`frame-ancestors` missing while `X-Frame-Options` exists.** They should agree; the CSP directive is the one browsers prefer.
- **Allow-listing a whole domain for embedding.** Any page on that domain can frame you; allow specific origins.
- **Consent screens frameable.** OAuth consent and permission prompts are prime clickjacking targets.
- **Assuming a POST with a CSRF token stops it.** The user's click carries the token; the frame is the problem.
- **No test.** Add a CI check asserting `frame-ancestors` on HTML responses.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **No `X-Frame-Options` or `frame-ancestors`** → your site can be embedded in an iframe and overlaid with invisible buttons. Attackers trick users into clicking "Delete Account" or "Transfer Funds".
- **`X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN`** → allows embedding by same origin. Use `DENY` unless you have a specific need.
