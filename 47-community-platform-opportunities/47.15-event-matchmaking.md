## 47.15 Event Matchmaking

### Subtopic Tree
- Definition: connecting attendees with the right people at events
- Matchmaking criteria (skills, interests, goals)

- Integration with community profiles
- The "networking anxiety" problem

### What Event Matchmaking Is
Event matchmaking is a **system that connects attendees with the right people at events**. It answers: **"Who should I talk to at this event?"**

The problem: networking is anxiety-inducing for many developers. They stand in a corner, talk to the one person they came with, and leave. Matchmaking **removes the anxiety** by suggesting who to talk to and why.

### Matchmaking Criteria
- **Shared skills** - "You both work with React Query"
- **Complementary skills** - "You're a frontend dev, they're a backend dev"
- **Shared goals** - "You both want to contribute to open source"
- **Shared interests** - "You both follow the same React thought leaders"
- **Experience gap** - "You're a junior, they're a senior open to mentoring"

### Pre-Event Matchmaking
Before the event, attendees receive a list of **3 suggested connections** with a brief explanation of why they should connect. The suggestions are mutual (both people see each other).

### At-Event Matchmaking
At the event, matchmaking continues:
- **Icebreaker prompts** - "Ask your neighbor: what's the most annoying React bug you've debugged?"
- **Table assignments** - mixed skill levels, shared interests
- **QR networking** - scan a QR code to exchange profiles

---

### Deeper Explanation: Matchmaking Turns Attendance Into Connections

Event matchmaking pairs attendees with people they should meet (shared interests, complementary skills, mentorship needs, hiring intent). It works when the signal is explicit and the recommendation is small: "three people to meet tonight", not a global graph.

Implementation notes: collect a lightweight interest profile per event (opt-in), match on overlapping tags plus complementary "looking for" fields, and keep it privacy-respecting (no contact details revealed without consent). In-person execution matters as much as the algorithm: a QR-based check-in, a printed badge with interests, or a table for matched groups converts matches into conversations.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Matching without consent.** Cold introductions feel intrusive; opt in per event.
- **Too many recommendations.** Ten matches is noise; three is actionable.
- **No follow-up path.** After the event, give a way to continue the conversation (with consent).
- **Ignoring complementary matches.** Pairing seniors with juniors is often more valuable than peers.
- **No feedback.** Without "did you meet?", the system cannot improve.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **No matchmaking** → networking is random. The same people talk to the same people.
- **No pre-event suggestions** → attendees don't know who to look for. They default to who they know.
- **No follow-up** → connections made at events are forgotten. Follow-up is where the value is.
- **No privacy controls** → attendees don't want to be matched with strangers.
