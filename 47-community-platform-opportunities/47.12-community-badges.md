### What a Community Badge Is
A community badge is a **digital credential** awarded for a specific community achievement. It answers: **"What has this person done?"** in a glance.

Badges are **portable reputation**. A community member can display their React Kolkata badges on their LinkedIn profile, their GitHub README, or their personal website.

### Badge Types

| Badge | Criteria | Display |
|-------|----------|---------|
| **First Talk** | Delivered first lightning or full talk | Profile, LinkedIn |
| **Speaker** | Delivered 3+ talks | Profile, LinkedIn |
| **Mentor** | Completed 5+ mentoring sessions | Profile, LinkedIn |
| **Contributor** | 5+ open source PRs merged | Profile, GitHub |
| **Organizer** | Organized 3+ events | Profile, LinkedIn |
| **Champion** | Top 3 contributor for a season | Profile, LinkedIn |
| **Parbo** | Project Parbon winner | Profile, LinkedIn |

### Badge Lifecycle
- **Earned**: member meets the criteria (verified)
- **Revoked**: if the contribution is found to be fraudulent (AI slop, plagiarism)

### Badge Design
Badges should be **visually consistent** with React Kolkata's brand. They should be small enough for a LinkedIn profile and large enough to be readable.

### Deeper Explanation: Badges Encode What The Community Values

A badge is a lightweight, verifiable signal of a specific contribution: first talk, ten answers, mentor for a season, event volunteer. They work when they are **specific, achievable, and earned** â€” and when the criteria are public so they cannot be gamed or disputed.

Implementation is simple (a rule, a trigger, a profile display), but the design decisions matter: cap the number of badge types, make each one earnable at multiple levels of effort, and let members hide them. Badges should also enable something (early registration, a mentor slot) rather than only decorate.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Badges for everything.** Meaningless badges train people to ignore all of them.
- **Unclear criteria.** Disputes and distrust; publish the rule and the evidence.
- **Status-only rewards.** Without a benefit, participation decays after the novelty.
- **No expiry or level.** A one-off badge from three years ago misrepresents current engagement.
- **Auto-awarding without consent.** Some members prefer privacy; allow opt-out.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **No badges** → contribution has no portable credential. Members can't prove their involvement.
- **Unverifiable badges** → anyone can claim a badge. The credential loses value.
- **No revocation** → fraudulent badges stay forever. The system loses trust.
- **No display integration** → badges are awarded but never seen. The recognition has no impact.
