## 47.13 Topic Voting

### Subtopic Tree

### What Topic Voting Is
Topic voting is a **community-driven mechanism for deciding what topics should be covered at future React Kolkata events**. It answers: **"What does the community want to learn about?"**

Instead of organizers guessing, the community votes. This ensures that events are **demand-driven**, not supply-driven.

### Voting Mechanism
```text
Topic Proposal (any member):
  "I want to learn about React Server Components"

Voting Period (2 weeks before event):
  - Upvote topics you want to hear about
  - Add comments explaining why

Results:
  - Top 3 topics are prioritized for the next event
  - Speakers are matched to winning topics
```

### The Topic Gap
The topic gap is the difference between **what the community wants to learn** and **what speakers are available to teach**. Topic voting makes this gap visible. If the community votes for "React Server Components" but no one is available to speak on it, organizers can **recruit a speaker** or **find an external expert**.

---

### Deeper Explanation: Voting Aligns Content With Demand

Topic voting lets the community decide what to cover next: proposed topics, upvotes, and status (proposed, scheduled, covered). It turns "what should we talk about?" from a guess into data, and it gives organisers evidence when approaching speakers.

The failure modes are familiar: duplicate proposals (search before proposing), votes with no commitment (interest without turnout), and dominant users skewing results (weight votes per member, not per click). ### Additional Pitfalls
- **Open proposals without merging duplicates.** The board fragments and voting is meaningless.
- **Votes treated as attendance guarantees.** Interests and availability differ; cap expectations.
- **No status updates.** Voters lose interest when nothing happens; publish outcomes.
- **Manipulable voting.** Rate-limit and require accounts.
- **Ignoring minority interests.** A vote-only model can exclude valuable niche topics; keep an editorial quota.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **No topic voting** → organizers guess what the community wants. Events miss the mark.
- **No results visibility** → members vote but never see the outcome. They stop voting.
- **No speaker matching** → winning topics don't get speakers. The vote was meaningless.
- **No voting window** → voting happens too late. Speakers don't have time to prepare.
