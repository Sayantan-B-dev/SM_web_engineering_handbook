### What Workshops Are
Workshops are **hands-on, interactive sessions** where participants build something together. Unlike meetup talks (presentation format), workshops are **learning-by-doing** - you leave with a working project, not just notes.

### The Miro Buildathon (August 2026)
The React Kolkata Meetup: Aug'26 Edition featured a **Miro Buildathon: "60 Minutes to Innovate"**. Participants had exactly **1 hour** to build anything they wanted using a blank Miro canvas. It was an **individual event** (no teams), and no Miro expertise was required. The goal was to showcase visual thinking skills - how participants used Miro to solve problems, organize workflows, or map out ideas.

### Deeper Explanation: Workshops Are Where Skill Transfer Happens

A workshop is the highest-bandwidth format in a community because everyone builds something. Two ways to get value: attend and actually type the exercises (rather than watching), or run one, which forces you to understand the topic better than any talk would.

For attendees: come with your own laptop configured, bring a real problem to apply the material to, and ask for the "why" behind each step. For presenters: keep the scope to one skill, prepare a starter repo so nobody blocks on setup, and include a stretch goal for the fast participants and a fallback path for the slow ones.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Workshops with setup as the content.** Half the time is spent debugging environments; ship a starter repo.
- **Too many topics.** One workshop should produce one working thing.
- **No exercises.** Passive watching does not transfer skill.
- **No catering for mixed skill levels.** Pair people up or provide tiered tasks.
- **No follow-up material.** Attendees forget 80% within a week; a repo and a recap help.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **No pre-requisites listed** → participants arrive without the right setup. Always publish setup instructions in advance.
- **No recording** → workshop content is lost after the event. Record and share.
- **Too much content, too little time** → participants leave overwhelmed. Focus on one outcome.
