### What Champions Do
- **Speak** at meetups
- **Mentor** Project Parbon participants
- **Organize** events and sessions
- **Contribute** to the open-source repository
- **Represent** React Kolkata at other conferences (e.g., Tapas Adhikary spoke at React Nexus 2026) 

### How to Become a Champion
1. **Attend** meetups regularly
2. **Ask questions** and participate in discussions
3. **Speak** at a lightning talk or full meetup
4. **Contribute** to the open-source repository
5. **Mentor** other community members
6. **Organize** a session or workshop

### Deeper Explanation: Champions Do The Unseen Work

Community champions are the people who make other people's participation possible: welcoming newcomers, keeping conversations on track, pairing people up, documenting decisions, and following up after events. That work is invisible in a talk count but is the reason communities survive.

The career benefit is real: organising and communication skills, visibility, and the trust that comes with responsibility.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Championing everything.** Unbounded volunteering leads to quitting; pick one recurring duty.
- **Doing the work silently.** Champions should be visible so others can help and learn.
- **Ignoring newcomers.** The single highest-value champion behaviour is greeting and including.
- **No handover.** Duties that live in one person's head disappear when they leave.
- **Conflict avoidance.** Unmanaged conflict drives away the people who make communities good.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Waiting to be invited** → champions volunteer. Speak up, contribute, organize.
- **Only consuming, never contributing** → community value is created by participation. Give back.
