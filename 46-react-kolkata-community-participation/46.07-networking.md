### The "Bring a Specific Question" Principle
ReactPlay's community organizers (whose model React Kolkata follows) emphasize a practical approach: **bring a specific question**. A developer investigating slow list rendering, for example, can explain the dataset, the observed behavior, and the approaches already tested. That gives other participants something concrete to discuss. 

### Following Up
Following up with a **small reproduction, an issue, or a short write-up** can turn an event conversation into shared technical work. The follow-up is where networking becomes collaboration. 

### The WhatsApp Community
React Kolkata maintains a **WhatsApp community** for real-time updates, discussions, and coordination around Project Parbon and other activities. 

### LinkedIn
React Kolkata has an active LinkedIn presence (2,000+ followers) where meetup announcements, speaker spotlights, and community updates are posted.

### Deeper Explanation: Be Useful, Then Stay in Touch

Community networking works the same way as professional networking: contribute first, then connect. In a local community the most valuable contributions are low-effort and high-signal: answering a question in the group, sharing a bug you solved, offering to review a beginner's PR, or co-presenting something small.

The follow-up discipline is what distinguishes a network from a stack of business cards: after each conversation, send one message referencing the specific thing you discussed, with a concrete offer or resource. Over a year, that produces a group of people who know what you do.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Collecting contacts without context.** Follow-ups that ignore the conversation feel automated.
- **Only reaching out when you need something.** Extraction is remembered.
- **Staying in your cohort.** Newcomers, organisers, and senior engineers are all worth talking to.
- **Over-promising.** "Let me introduce you to my CTO" is a commitment; only say it if you will.
- **No memory system.** Keep a short note per person: what they work on, what they need.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Only talking to people you came with** → the most valuable connections are with people you don't know. Approach someone new. 
- **No follow-up** → connections fade within weeks. Follow up within 48 hours.
- **Networking only when you need something** → give first. Answer questions, share resources, offer help.
