### What Community Quizzes Are
Community quizzes are **low-stakes knowledge tests** that make learning fun and identify knowledge gaps. They can be **live** (at meetups) or **async** (online, self-paced).

### Quiz Topics (Typical React Quiz)
- **React Basics**: What is React? Who developed it? What is JSX? 
- **Hooks**: What is `useState` used for? What is `useEffect`? 
- **Rendering**: What is conditional rendering? What does `map()` do? 
- **State Management**: Why are callback props used? 

### Quiz Formats

| Format | When | How |
|--------|------|-----|
| **Live quiz** | At meetups | Kahoot-style, projected on screen |
| **Async quiz** | Online | Google Forms, self-paced |
| **Team quiz** | At meetups | Teams of 3-4, competitive |

---

### Deeper Explanation: Retrieval Practice Beats Reading

Quizzes work because recall is what consolidates knowledge; reading feels productive but is weak for retention. A community quiz (in a meetup or a group chat) is also a diagnostic: the questions people get wrong reveal what the ecosystem misunderstands, which is excellent talk material.

To get value from participating: attempt answers before checking, note each concept you could not explain confidently, and turn those into learning tasks. For organisers: ask questions about mechanisms and trade-offs, not trivia, and always explain the answer.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Trivia instead of understanding.** API-name questions teach nothing; "why does this fail" questions do.
- **Looking up answers immediately.** It removes the retrieval benefit.
- **No explanations.** Wrong answers without reasoning leave misconceptions intact.
- **Winners-only framing.** It discourages the people who most need the practice.
- **No follow-up.** Concepts missed in the quiz should become the next study session.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Making quizzes too hard** → beginners feel discouraged. Start with fundamentals.
- **No explanations** → quizzes without answer explanations don't teach anything. Explain every answer.
