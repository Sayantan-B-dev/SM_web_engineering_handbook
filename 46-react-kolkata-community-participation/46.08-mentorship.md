### Deeper Explanation: Mentoring Is Structured Time, Not Advice

Mentorship in a community works when it has a goal, a cadence, and a boundary. A mentor's job is to help someone make decisions, not to do their work: ask what they have tried, help them frame the problem, and connect them to resources and people. Thirty minutes every two weeks with a written agenda beats improvised advice.

For mentees, the expectations that make it work: bring a specific question or blocker, show the work you have done, and respect the given time. Find mentors for *decisions*, not for tutorials â€” blog posts and docs handle tutorials better.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **No agenda.** Sessions drift into casual chat without progress.
- **Doing the work for them.** It creates dependency and hides the learning.
- **Vague goals.** "Learn React" needs to become "ship this feature".
- **Unbounded availability.** Always-on mentorship leads to burnout; agree on limits.
- **No ending.** Define the goal and celebrate it; a mentoring relationship can conclude cleanly.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Not applying for Project Parbon** → the mentorship prize is one of the most valuable outcomes. Submit your project. 
- **Not asking for mentorship outside Project Parbon** → community mentors are accessible. Ask questions in the WhatsApp community or at meetups.
- **Mentorship without follow-through** → the mentor-mentee relationship requires effort from both sides. Come prepared with specific questions.
