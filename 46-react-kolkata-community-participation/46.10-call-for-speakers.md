React Kolkata regularly issues a **Call for Speakers** - an open invitation for community members to propose talks for upcoming meetups. Speaker announcements are made on LinkedIn and through the WhatsApp community.

### Deeper Explanation: CFP Is A Skill You Can Practise

Responding to a call for speakers is a writing exercise with a deadline: the abstract must promise something specific, explain why the audience will care, and show that the speaker has done the work. Reviewers are reading dozens of abstracts, so the first two sentences decide.

```text
Title: Why your React app feels slow (and what to measure first)

Abstract:
Most performance work starts with the wrong question. In this 25-minute talk, I
will show how to find the real bottleneck with a trace and a budget, using a
checkout page that went from 4.2 s to 1.1 s LCP on mid-tier Android. You will
leave with a repeatable method: measure, attribute, fix, guard. Includes the
before/after numbers and the two changes that produced 80% of the gain.

Audience: intermediate React developers. Prerequisites: none.
Speaker bio: <one line>
```

Practical tips: propose the talk you can give in three weeks, submit to multiple CFPs, and keep a reusable abstract file so you can respond quickly.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Vague abstracts.** "Let's explore React performance" gives the reviewer nothing to select.
- **No audience or level.** Reviewers cannot place it in a schedule.
- **Proposing a talk you have not begun.** Selection creates deadline pressure and a mediocre result.
- **Ignoring the CFP's questions.** It signals you will be hard to work with.
- **No proof of speaking ability.** Link a lightning talk or a recorded demo.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Thinking you're not experienced enough** → lightning talks are designed for first-time speakers. You don't need 10 years of experience to share one idea.
- **No abstract** → speakers are selected based on clear, specific talk proposals. Write a short abstract with a clear takeaway.
- **Not rehearsing** → even a 5-minute lightning talk needs practice. Rehearse at least 3 times.
