### Deeper Explanation: Partnerships Extend Reach By Sharing Surfaces

Community partnerships (with companies, universities, other meetups) work when each side brings a complementary asset: venues and sponsorship, audiences and speakers, mentors and projects. The mechanics that keep them healthy are simple and often skipped: a named contact on both sides, a written understanding of what each provides, and a review after each event.

For organisers, the useful rule is to ask for specifics rather than money: a venue, a speaker, a mentor slot, a project brief, or a recruitment slot advertised with transparency. ### Additional Pitfalls
- **Sponsorship that shapes content.** It costs credibility; keep editorial control.
- **No named contact.** Partnerships decay when they depend on "the company".
- **One-sided value.** Partners leave when they get nothing measurable.
- **No recap or review.** Both sides forget what was agreed and repeat the negotiation.
- **Exclusive arrangements.** They shrink the community's options; prefer open, parallel relationships.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **No partnership strategy** → communities operate in silos. Cross-pollination grows both.
- **Partnership without value exchange** → one-sided partnerships don't last. Both sides must benefit.
