### Deeper Explanation: Getting Value From A Meetup Requires A Plan

Meetups pay off when you arrive with a goal and leave with a follow-up. A simple plan: one question you want answered (ask it in the Q&A or to a speaker afterwards), one person you want to meet, and one thing you will write down. That converts a passive evening into a concrete gain.

For talks, the takeaway matters more than the depth: note the problem, the approach, and one number or lesson you can apply next week. For conversations, ask about what people are building and what is frustrating them â€” that is where both learning and opportunities live.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Leaving immediately after the talk.** The hallway conversation is where the value is.
- **Only talking to people you know.** It reproduces your existing network.
- **No follow-up.** A LinkedIn message the next day is what turns a chat into a connection.
- **Collecting notes you never revisit.** Write one action item, not a transcript.
- **Never asking questions.** Asking publicly is how you become known.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Not registering in advance** → offline meetups have limited seats. Register on Luma as soon as registration opens.
- **Only attending online** → you miss the networking and mentorship that happens in person.
- **Not bringing a specific question** → the most valuable meetup conversations start with a concrete problem. Bring a bug you're debugging or an architecture decision you're weighing.
