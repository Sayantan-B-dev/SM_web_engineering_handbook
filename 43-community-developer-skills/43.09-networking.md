### Networking as Value, Not Spam
**Clarify your goals** (learning, roles, domains) and pick the right channels:
- **GitHub/communities** for technical connections
- **LinkedIn** for visibility
- **Meetups/conferences** for deeper relationships
- **Lead with value** - share resources, answer questions, contribute before asking.

### The Channels

| Channel | Purpose | Best for |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **GitHub** | Technical collaboration | Open source contributions, code reviews |
| **LinkedIn** | Professional visibility | Career growth, industry connections |
| **Meetups** | Local community | Deeper relationships, speaking practice |
| **Conferences** | Industry-wide | Learning, speaking, meeting maintainers |
| **Discord/Slack** | Real-time community | Quick questions, daily interaction |
| **Dev.to / Hashnode** | Content creation | Building reputation, teaching |
| **Twitter/X** | Tech Twitter | Following industry trends, networking |

### Building in Public
**Join a public challenge or open-source sprint.** Ship small, frequently, and talk about your progress. #100DaysOfCode, #BuildInPublic, or even a weekly "what I built" thread creates rhythm and visibility.

### The Developmental Network
A developmental network is the **small group of people who take an active interest in your career**. It may include a mentor, a manager, peers, a former boss, and someone outside your organization. Networking is not about collecting contacts - it's about building a network of people who genuinely care about your growth.

### Giving Before Asking
The most effective networking is **giving first**. Answer questions on Stack Overflow. Review a PR. Share a resource. Write a blog post. The people you help will remember you when you need help.

---

### Deeper Explanation: Build Relationships Through Work

Engineering networking is weakest when it is transactional. The durable approach is to be useful in public: answer questions in community channels, write up problems you solved, contribute to projects you use, and speak about your work. Relationships follow repeated useful contact, not business cards.

Practical habits: contribute to a community you actually use (one hour a week), write short public notes on what you learned (they compound), follow up on conversations with a specific offer ("I have a script for that, want it?"), and remember people's context (what they are working on and struggling with).

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Only reaching out when you need something.** It reads as extraction and closes doors.
- **Collecting contacts instead of relationships.** A list of names is not a network.
- **Waiting for a formal event.** Contributions in issue threads are networking too.
- **No memory of context.** Follow-ups that ignore what someone said feel automated.
- **Over-promising in the moment.** Say what you will actually deliver.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Networking only when you need something** → transactional and off-putting. Give first.
- **No follow-up** → connections fade within weeks. Follow up within 48 hours.
- **Only networking online** → deeper relationships happen in person. Attend meetups.
- **No clear goals** → you collect contacts but build no relationships. Clarify what you want.
- **Spamming LinkedIn** → you become a nuisance. Lead with value.
