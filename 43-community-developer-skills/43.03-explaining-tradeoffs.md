## 43.3 Explaining Tradeoffs

### Subtopic Tree
- Definition: a design decision where improving one property degrades another

- Articulating the upside and downside
- Quantifying trade-offs (when possible)
- Trade-off matrices
- Decision under uncertainty

### What a Trade-off Is
A trade-off is a **design decision where improving one measurable property of the system necessarily degrades another**. Every architectural decision is a trade-off. There is no perfect solution - only solutions that are better or worse for specific constraints.

### The Eight Fundamental Trade-offs
There are **eight fundamental trade-offs** that must be considered before making any software requirement or design decision:

| Trade-off | Question |
|-----------|----------|
| **Development cost vs product performance** | Does more performance justify more development expense? |
| **Time-to-market vs product performance** | Do we ship sooner with less performance, or later with more? |
| **Development speed vs development cost** | Can we go faster by spending more? |
| **Product cost vs product performance** | Does higher performance justify a higher price? |
| **Complexity vs simplicity** | Does the added capability justify the added complexity? |
| **Build vs buy** | Do we build it ourselves or pay for a solution? |
| **Centralization vs decentralization** | Do we centralize control or distribute it? |
| **Consistency vs availability** | Do we guarantee consistency or availability during failures? |

### Articulating Trade-offs: The Structure
Every trade-off explanation should follow this structure:

```markdown
## Decision: Use Redis for session storage

## What we gain
- Sub-millisecond session lookups (vs 5-10ms for PostgreSQL)
- Automatic TTL-based session expiry
- Reduced database load (sessions move off PostgreSQL)

## What we give up
- Redis becomes a single point of failure (need Redis cluster for HA)
- Session data is in-memory; a Redis restart loses all sessions
- Additional infrastructure to operate

## Why we're choosing this
- Session lookups are on the critical path for every authenticated request
- The PostgreSQL load from sessions is 30% of total queries
- We can add Redis cluster later if HA becomes necessary

## What would change our mind
- If Redis operational complexity exceeds the benefit
- If we need sessions to survive Redis restarts without a cluster
```

### Trade-off Matrices
When comparing multiple options, a trade-off matrix makes the decision transparent:

| Option | Performance | Complexity | Cost | Team Familiarity | Risk |
|--------|------------|-----------|------|-----------------|------|
| **PostgreSQL sessions** | 5-10ms | Low | $0 | High | Low |
| **Redis sessions** | <1ms | Medium | $50/mo | Medium | Medium |
| **JWT (stateless)** | <1ms | High | $0 | Medium | High (revocation) |
| **DynamoDB sessions** | 2-5ms | Medium | Pay-per-use | Low | Medium |

### "Not Choosing" as a Valid Option
Sometimes the right decision is to **not decide yet**. If the constraints are unclear, the requirements are unstable, or the team lacks information, deferring the decision is a legitimate trade-off. Document why you're deferring and what would trigger a decision.

---

### Deeper Explanation: Name Both Sides Or Nobody Believes You

A trade-off statement is credible when it names what you lose: "we gain developer velocity and lose the ability to isolate failures". Fake trade-offs ("this is better and also faster and simpler") are a signal that the analysis is incomplete. The structure that works: option, what you gain, what you give up, why the balance is right here, and what would change your mind.

```markdown
## Decision: Redis for session storage

**What we gain**: sub-millisecond session reads, no per-request writes to Postgres,
and a store that can be scaled independently.

**What we give up**: a second stateful dependency, an eviction policy that can log
everyone out if misconfigured, and no transactional guarantee with user records.

**Why now**: session reads are 34% of database QPS, and the profile is 90% reads.

**What would change our mind**: a need for transactional session updates
(for example, credit-consuming sessions) or a hard requirement for a single datastore.
```

### Additional Pitfalls
- **One-sided analysis.** It reads as advocacy and invites adversarial review.
- **Costs without mitigations.** Every risk should name the plan that addresses it.
- **No decision criteria.** Without them the trade-off is a preference.
- **Ignoring second-order costs.** Operational load, hiring, and migration effort are real.
- **No revisit trigger.** Decisions without expiry get re-litigated or over-applied.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Presenting only the upside** → you've made a sales pitch, not a trade-off analysis. The team doesn't know what they're giving up.
- **No quantification** → "Redis is faster" is not a trade-off. "Redis is <1ms vs 5-10ms" is.
- **Decision paralysis** → analyzing forever without deciding. Set a deadline for the decision.
- **Ignoring the cost of indecision** → every day without a decision is a day of technical debt.
- **No "what would change our mind"** → the decision is treated as permanent. Document the conditions that would trigger a revisit.
