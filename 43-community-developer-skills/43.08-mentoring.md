### Why Mentoring Matters
A good mentorship program **improves retention and helps junior engineers grow quickly into developers who ship higher-quality code**. Effective mentorship helps junior developers improve technical, problem-solving, and collaboration skills more quickly.

### Structured 30-60-90 Day Plans
Structured 30-60-90 day mentorship plans enhance onboarding and growth.

| Phase | Focus | Goals |
|-------|-------|-------|
| **Days 1-30** | Onboarding | Set up dev environment, ship first small PR, understand codebase |
| **Days 31-60** | Ownership | Own a feature end-to-end, participate in code reviews |
| **Days 61-90** | Autonomy | Lead a small project, mentor another new hire |

### Guiding, Not Answering
Great mentors **guide rather than provide immediate answers**. When a junior asks "How do I fix this?", the mentor's response should be a question: "What have you tried so far?" or "What do you think is happening?"

```text
❌ Answer-first: "You need to use a Map instead of an Object."

✅ Guide-first: "What's the difference between a Map and an Object?
   When would you choose one over the other?"
```

### Code Reviews as Mentoring
Code reviews are the **primary mentoring mechanism**. The mentor reviews the junior's code with explanatory rationale, sample solutions backed by standards, and a constructive tone.

```markdown
# ✅ Mentoring code review comment
"This function works, but it does two things: validation and database
logic. Consider separating them into two functions. Here's an example
from our codebase: `validateUserInput()` and `saveUser()`.
This makes each function easier to test and reuse."

# ❌ Non-mentoring code review comment
"This is wrong."
```

### Mentoring in the AI Era
Mentorship in 2026 looks different. Junior developers have AI tools at their fingertips. The mentor's role isn't just to teach code anymore - it's to **teach critical thinking and ensure AI doesn't mislead them**.

Key mentoring practices in the AI era:
- **Understand the "Why", not just the output**: Don't just copy-paste AI code. If you can't explain what every line does, don't ship it.
- **Spotting the hallucinations**: Teaching juniors how to code-review AI outputs and question assumptions.
- **Building engineering intuition**: Fostering the problem-solving mindset that no LLM can replicate.
- **AI-off sessions**: Sessions where juniors solve problems without AI, to build fundamentals.

### The Developmental Network (One Mentor Is Not Enough)
A **developmental network** is the small group of people who take an active interest in your career and help it along. It may include a mentor, a manager, a few peers, a former boss, and someone outside your organization. No single mentor can provide everything.

### Mentoring as a System, Not a Trait
Mentoring is a **system**. You assign it. You set expectations. You recognize it in performance reviews. You make it as visible as shipping a feature. When mentoring is formalized, it improves retention and accelerates junior growth.

---

### Deeper Explanation: Mentoring Is Structured Independence

Good mentoring transfers judgement, not answers. The model that works: set the goal together, let the mentee do the work, review the reasoning, and increase scope as trust grows. Asking questions ("what have you ruled out, and how?") teaches more than providing solutions, and the mentee owns the outcome.

Practical structure: weekly or biweekly 30-minute sessions with an agenda the mentee writes, a shared document of goals and progress, and explicit expectations about what will be reviewed. Reverse mentoring (they teach you something) keeps it a two-way relationship rather than a hierarchy.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Solving instead of guiding.** They become dependent and you become a bottleneck.
- **No agenda.** Sessions drift into status updates and lose their purpose.
- **Vague goals.** "Get better at React" is unmeasurable; define a concrete outcome.
- **Feedback delayed until reviews.** Course corrections work best in the moment.
- **No endings.** Mentoring relationships should graduate; state the goal and celebrate it.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **No structured plan** → the junior flounders. A 30-60-90 day plan gives direction.
- **Answer-first mentoring** → the junior never learns to think. Guide with questions.
- **No code review mentoring** → code reviews become gatekeeping, not teaching.
- **One mentor for everything** → the mentor burns out. Build a developmental network.
- **Ignoring AI in mentoring** → juniors ship AI-generated code they don't understand. Teach critical review.
