### Preparing for Q&A
**Anticipate the top 5 questions** you're likely to get. For each, prepare a 30-second answer. The best Q&A sessions are the ones you've already rehearsed.

```markdown
## Anticipated Questions

**Q: Why not use GraphQL?**
A: GraphQL adds a gateway and schema registry. For our use case
(5 endpoints, TypeScript monorepo), tRPC gives us type safety
without the operational overhead.

**Q: What about non-TypeScript clients?**
A: We have one legacy Python client. We'll keep a REST endpoint
for that specific use case and migrate everything else to tRPC.

**Q: How long will migration take?**
A: 3 weeks for the core endpoints, plus 1 week buffer.
We'll migrate incrementally with both running in parallel.
```

### Handling Hostile Questions
A hostile question is not an attack on you. It's usually an attack on the idea, or a genuine concern expressed poorly.

```text
❌ Defensive: "That's not true. GraphQL is way more complex."

✅ Constructive: "That's a fair concern. GraphQL is more flexible,
   but it adds a gateway and schema registry. For our team size
   and use case, tRPC gives us the type safety without the overhead.
   I'm happy to walk through the specific trade-offs after the talk."
```

### Handling Questions You Don't Know
```text
❌ "I don't know."

✅ "I don't know the answer to that. Let me find out and follow up.
   Can I get your email or Slack handle?"
```

The "I'll follow up" technique is not a failure. It's a sign of honesty and professionalism. No one expects you to know everything.

### Time Management
- **Reserve 5 minutes for Q&A.** If your talk is 30 minutes, prepare 25 minutes of content.
- **If Q&A runs long**, politely say: "We're running low on time. Let's take two more questions and then continue offline."
- **If there are no questions**, have a prepared "starter question" for yourself: "A question I often get is..."

---

### Deeper Explanation: Questions Are The Real Test

Q&A reveals whether you understand your own decision. Preparation is straightforward: list the ten questions you would ask as a sceptic (cost, scale, migration, security, failure modes, alternatives), write one-line answers, and decide honestly which ones you do not know â€” "I do not know, I will find out and follow up by Thursday" is a strong answer.

Handling mechanics: repeat or restate the question (it helps the room and buys thinking time), answer concisely, then check whether it landed; for hostile or vague questions, ask for the underlying concern; park deep dives into a follow-up conversation instead of derailing the session.

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Answering a different question.** Restate to confirm before answering.
- **Bluffing.** Technical audiences notice, and it costs more than admitting uncertainty.
- **Letting one person dominate.** Park it with "let us take that offline and share notes".
- **No follow-up loop.** Promised answers must actually arrive.
- **Running out of time because Q&A was unplanned.** Budget it explicitly.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **No Q&A preparation** → the first hostile question throws you off. Anticipate the top 5.
- **Getting defensive** → you lose the audience. Stay calm and constructive.
- **Pretending to know** → the audience sees through it. "I'll follow up" is better.
- **Q&A running over time** → the next speaker is delayed. Moderate firmly.
- **No starter question** → awkward silence. Have one prepared.
