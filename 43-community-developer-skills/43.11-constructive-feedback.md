### The Observation → Suggestion → Reason Framework
The most effective constructive feedback follows a three-part structure:

```
Observation: "I noticed this method is doing both validation and database logic."
Suggestion: "It might be worth separating those concerns into different functions."
Reason: "That would make testing and maintenance easier."
```

### I-Messages
Use "I" instead of "you" to signal opinion, not universal truth.

```text
❌ "You're doing this wrong."
✅ "I found this confusing. Could we add a comment explaining the intent?"

❌ "This code is bad."
✅ "I think this function could be simplified. Here's an alternative."
```

### Specific vs Vague Feedback

| ❌ Vague | ✅ Specific |
|---------|------------|
| "This is confusing." | "The variable `data` is used for three different things. Could we rename it to `userData`, `postData`, and `commentData`?" |
| "This could be better." | "Consider extracting lines 42-58 into a `validateInput()` function. It would reduce the complexity of `handleSubmit`." |
| "Good job." | "I liked how you handled the null case with optional chaining. That's a pattern we should use more." |

### Balancing Positive and Critical Feedback
Acknowledge good work explicitly. "Nice error handling here" or "I liked how you structured this test". Feedback that is only critical feels like an attack, even when it's constructive.

### Handling Defensiveness
- **Don't argue.** Let the person process the feedback.
- **Ask questions.** "What's your perspective on this?"
- **Focus on the code, not the person.** "This function has a bug" not "You made a mistake."
- **Offer to pair.** "Let's look at this together."

### Additional Pitfalls
- **Vague feedback.** "Be more proactive" is unactionable; name the behaviour and the moment.
- **Feedback sandwiches.** The criticism gets lost between compliments.
- **Delayed feedback.** Weeks later it becomes archaeology; be timely.
- **Only negative feedback.** It makes feedback itself feel like an attack.
- **Public criticism of a person.** Address private behaviour privately; discuss systems in public.

### Real-World Deal-Breakers
- **Vague feedback** → the author doesn't know what to fix. Be specific.
- **Only critical feedback** → the author feels attacked. Acknowledge good work.
- **"You" statements** → triggers defensiveness. Use "I" messages.
- **No explanation** → the author doesn't learn. Explain the "why."
- **Public criticism** → humiliating. Give critical feedback in private.
